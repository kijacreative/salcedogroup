01

GIS planning

Spatial data that supports early alternatives, agency meetings and community studies.

  • Project base mapping
  • Community access studies
  • Infrastructure impact review
  • Alternatives mapping

SGI is working with the Trinity Park Conservancy on an urban design initiative studying access, equity and opportunity for the communities connecting to Harold Simmons Park, including the infrastructure impacts of pedestrian, bicycle and vehicular connections.

Both principals are trained in GIS: Luis M. Salcedo holds an M.S. in GIS from UT Dallas, and Michael A. Salcedo holds an A.A.S. in GIS from TCC.

02

GIS mapping

Survey, utility and parcel data combined on one base for every discipline on the job.

  • Survey and parcel data integration
  • Utility mapping
  • Right-of-way mapping
  • Exhibits for agencies and stakeholders

Michael A. Salcedo’s experience includes GIS planning and mapping alongside surveying and design, so the map and the plans come from the same data.

On the Hi Line Connection, mapping supported coordination among City departments, TxDOT, the North Texas Tollway Authority, ONCOR Electric and private real-estate stakeholders.

03

Spatial analysis

Analysis that answers siting, drainage, access and utility questions before design.

  • Access and connectivity analysis
  • Utility conflict screening
  • Drainage area analysis
  • Siting studies

For Harold Simmons Park, SGI is assessing the major utility conflicts that could affect the park’s connections, and informing the infrastructure impacts of pedestrian, bicycle and vehicular access.

Questions

How does GIS help early planning?

It puts parcels, utilities, floodplains and access on one map, so alternatives can be compared before design starts.

Do SGI’s principals have GIS training?

Yes. Luis M. Salcedo holds an M.S. in GIS from UT Dallas and a GIS certificate from the Bruton Center; Michael A. Salcedo holds an A.A.S. and certificate in GIS from TCC.

What data goes on a GIS base?

Typically the survey, parcels, right of way, utilities and floodplains, so every discipline works from the same map.

Does SGI map right of way?

Yes. Right-of-way mapping was part of SGI’s scope on the Hi Line Connection.

What questions does spatial analysis answer?

Where conflicts, constraints and opportunities sit before design: utility conflicts, drainage areas, access gaps and siting.

Where has SGI used it?

On Harold Simmons Park, to assess utility conflicts and the access impacts of the park’s community connections.
